I've been building software for more than fourteen years, and I'm still the person who gets excited when a new
framework drops, a browser ships a new API, or a build gets a few seconds faster. I like knowing how things work
under the hood, and I like the moment when a hard problem finally clicks.

What I haven't done is write much of it down. This site is my fix for that.

## A career worth writing about

My path has taken me through very different engineering cultures, each with its own way of building:

- **Intel**, where I started out building internal applications. I learned CI/CD, test-driven development, and what it takes to lead a team of developers and teach new technologies to others.
- **Amazon**, where I worked on seller and partner support platforms, and later **AWS Marketplace**, designing large-scale distributed services.
- **Microsoft**, where I built address bar features for Microsoft Edge, and today lead Finance and Sports experiences across Bing, Copilot, and MSN in the Microsoft AI organization.

Along the way I moved from Costa Rica to Vancouver, went from Angular and .NET to React and TypeScript, and from
on-prem servers to the cloud. Each step taught me something I wish I'd had in writing before I started it.
That's the kind of thing I want to share here.

## What I'll write about

- **The craft of front-end engineering.** React and TypeScript architecture, reusable components, and the details that make an interface feel fast and solid.
- **Data visualization.** How to turn numbers into something people actually understand, especially in finance and sports, where I spend my days.
- **Accessibility.** Building interfaces that work for everyone isn't a checklist at the end; it's part of the craft from the start.
- **Lessons from big companies.** How different teams approach design reviews, on-call, testing, and shipping at scale, and what I'd take from each.
- **New tech I'm trying.** Tools, frameworks, and AI-assisted development, with honest notes on what works and what doesn't.

## Learning by writing

The biggest reason is selfish: writing is how I learn. Explaining something clearly forces me to understand it
properly. The gaps I can hide in my head show up immediately on the page. If a post helps someone else along
the way, that's the best possible bonus.

I'm also bilingual, and I hope to write some posts in Spanish. Plenty of great engineers in Latin America are
looking for content in their own language, and I'd like to contribute to that.
